# 034 — The Flash Crash  (epic / force)

Scene beats from lore: the book was a rumor for six minutes; prices nobody admits to; almost normal by breakfast.

> Museum-quality oil painting on aged canvas, dark epic fantasy meets financial mythology. An immense column of black glass shattering mid-fall in a dark exchange hall, its upper half already dissolved into a suspended cloud of splinters, each shard catching ember light. Below, a tiny city of lit desks glows on, unaware. One large engraved bronze bell hangs silent above the destruction. Palette strictly black, bronze, oxidised gold, ember orange and bone white, with one narrow cold blue rim light separating the silhouette from the dark and a single hard warm key light raking from the upper left. Painterly visible brushwork, impasto in the highlights, deep chiaroscuro falloff, aged varnish, fine canvas grain. Centred composition with the event occupying the upper two thirds of the frame, the lower third falling away into quiet near-black empty shadow with no detail. The painting runs fully to every edge of the image with no painted frame, no border, no matte, no vignette band — the outermost edges are dark, uniform and undisturbed open shadow. All carved and engraved surfaces are plain geometric ornament only. Absolutely no text, no letters, no words, no numerals, no runes, no glyphs, no inscriptions, no currency symbols, no signature, no watermark, no logos anywhere in the image.
